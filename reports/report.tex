\section{Overview}
We built a dataset for a small language model that answers M.Tech course advising questions: what is still left
for my degree, can I register for a course next term, and which electives should I take. Scope is four programmes in
the CS and EE departments, MTech-CSE, MTech-AI, MTech-EE and MTech-ICDT, each with a thesis and a coursework track
(\f{docs/DECISIONS.md} D1 to D3). A rule engine built from the institute's own documents computes every gold answer, so
each label points back to a source page. A local LLM (Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct) paraphrases questions and
justifications and two humans audit those paraphrases. Headline numbers: 104 courses and 77 rules with provenance,
180 synthetic students, 600 advisory pairs (200 per intent), 1609 verified augmented children, 80 doubly labelled
audit items, a frozen gold test set of 76 items, and \nforms{} real Google Form questions as a shifted test set.

\section{Part A: Collection}
\textbf{Sources and provenance.} The raw layer holds eight academic advisories (Adv5, Adv8, Adv17, Adv24, Adv42,
Adv60, Adv\_05, Adv\_08), the revised CSE M.Tech course structure 2025-26, the Sem-I 2026-27 timetable (xlsx) and four
pages of the EE M.Tech Google Site. Each file has a sha256 in \f{data/raw/sources/source_metadata.json}. Every course
and rule record carries \f{source_doc} plus \f{source_page}, \f{source_row} or \f{source_section}, and a
\f{record_sha256}; every rule also stores a verbatim quote that \f{build_rules} checks against the page text.
\f{data/metadata.csv} has \nmeta{} rows (\nmetaraw{} raw, \nmetaproc{} processed), one per sample, with source,
page, collector, tool, version and content hash.

\textbf{Course plans.} Plan links come from the timetable's ``Link To Course Plan'' cell. For the 50 CS/ES codes,
15 plans downloaded, 12 links need an IITGN login (Google sign-in page, not bypassed) and 23 codes have no link. For
the 25 EE codes, 7 downloaded, 4 are login-gated and 14 have no link. That gives 22 plan files in
\f{data/raw/course_plans/}.

\textbf{Catalog and rules.} \f{catalog_raw.json} keeps 136 records with their defects (duplicates, the
``Algrithms'' typo, float credits, section suffixes). \f{clean_catalog} turns them into 104 courses (67 basket, 15
open elective, 6 stream core, 2 compulsory, 6 requirement, 8 UG background) and logs all 78 changes in
\f{cleaning_changes.csv}. \f{rules.json} has 77 rules, 20 of them EE-specific (\f{ee.*}); one, maximum credits per
semester, is marked \f{not_stated} because no source gives it.

\textbf{Prerequisites finding.} No source states an enforceable PG prerequisite: 103 of 104 courses have
\f{prereq_source: none_stated} and one has a manual entry with an empty list (CS 613, ``Prerequisite (Optional)''
with UG courses ``or equivalent''). So the engine has two tiers: hard prerequisites (empty in practice, but checked)
and \f{recommended_background}, which 3 courses carry. The prerequisite graph has 104 nodes and 9 edges, all of type
recommended, no missing codes and no cycles. D4 stores UG codes named in plans as recommended background; D5 (decided
by hem436) says UG-level background never warns an M.Tech student, because they did their UG elsewhere.

\textbf{Synthetic students, consent, data flow.} The engine generates 180 students from seed 203 (CSE 64, EE 51, AI
41, ICDT 24; 92 thesis, 88 coursework) with 1422 transcript entries and 0 rule violations. The institute documents
are public regulations used for non-commercial coursework and are not redistributed; course plans were saved only
where the link opened without a login, and instructor names are not copied. Form respondents tick a required
consent box, no emails or names are collected, the Google timestamp is dropped and contact details in free text are
removed (\f{docs/CONSENT_AND_LICENSING.md}). Data flows sources $\to$ \f{extract_sources} $\to$
\f{fetch_course_plans}/\f{fetch_ee_pages} $\to$ \f{build_catalog} $\to$ \f{clean_catalog} and \f{build_rules} $\to$
rule engine $\to$ queries $\to$ Label Studio (\f{docs/DATA_FLOW.md}).

\section{Part B: Validation}
The suite has 25 checks (V01 to V25) written with Pandera schemas, networkx graph checks, rapidfuzz near-duplicate
titles and the rule engine itself. It runs on three stages. On the raw catalog 10 checks apply: 7 fail, 2 pass,
1 warns. On processed data all 25 run: 24 pass and V10 warns about 2 near-duplicate titles of different codes, kept
on purpose (Table~\ref{tab:ba}). On a corrupted copy (seed 209) all 15 injected faults are caught, including a
prerequisite cycle, an unknown prerequisite, total credits changed from 82 to 80, a below-600 cap breach and
train/test leakage; one planned fault (per-semester credit cap) was not injected because no cap is stated.

Validation alone did not catch everything; human review of the gold answers found two engine bugs. (1) While
labelling the first item, hem436 saw ``Other baskets (): 0 credits earned'' for an MTech-AI student: the engine took
other baskets from the student's own programme, which for MTech-AI has one stream. The fix follows the CSE structure
(MTech-AI follows the AI-ML curriculum) and changed 100 of 600 gold answers, all MTech-AI. (2) hem436 noticed
eligibility answers telling M.Tech students they lacked IITGN UG courses (for example ES 666 needing ES 112). D5 removed
these warnings and changed 53 gold answers, 0 query texts. Both fixes have regression tests and happened before any
finished annotation was affected. Because the AI fix also changes how course roles are assigned (it would have
changed 12 of the 180 synthetic students, all MTech-AI), the transcript generator keeps the old basket view (\f{role_of(..., own_programme_baskets=True)}) so the labelled students are
reproduced exactly, while all gold answers use the fixed rule.

\section{Part C: Labelling and agreement}
\textbf{Task.} Each item shows a student summary, the template query, the LLM paraphrase, the rule-engine gold answer
and the LLM-rewritten justification. Annotators fill three levels in a fixed order: decision (accept, edit, reject),
then one error category (none, hallucinated\_course, wrong\_fact\_or\_policy, meaning\_drift,
incomplete\_justification, fluency, ambiguous\_query), then the edit text. The decision comes first so the category
list cannot steer it. Two annotators (A1 = SMH, A2 = MSB) labelled the same 40 items per round in Label Studio 1.23.2,
two disjoint rounds, all drawn from the test split (seed 208). Round 1 used guidelines v1; after it we wrote v2
(seven rules, Appendix~\ref{app:c}), each tied to round-1 disagreements, and used it for round 2.

\textbf{Disagreements.} Round 1 was mostly about strictness, not category confusion: A2 accepted 34 of 40 items and
A1 24, and in 11 of the 17 disagreements A1 flagged an item A2 accepted (Figure~\ref{fig:conf}). Causes: vague
``almost there'' justifications, a contradicted number inside fluent text, the edit-or-reject threshold, and A1
correcting a correct gold answer from memory. In round 2 the main split left was wrong numbers in fluent text,
plus the paraphraser turning ``coursework track'' into ``workshop track''. Kappa rose in both levels, but the CIs
overlap and the rounds use different items, so the gain is in the expected direction, not conclusive.

\textbf{Adjudication.} 50 items agreed on both levels and needed no adjudication. For the other 30 (17 + 13; round 2
also includes one item where both chose edit with different texts), the assistant drafted a proposed label and a
gold-grounded edit for the 29 disagreements, with a one-line reason each. A1 and A2 reviewed every proposal together
on a review page and made the call: 28 approved, 1 overridden (\f{decided_by} ``A1+A2''). The extra item was decided
by A1. No item stayed unresolved. Final audit outcome over 80 items: 55 accept, 21 edit, 4 reject. The gold test set
is the 76 accepted or edited items (27 eligibility, 27 elective recommendation, 22 remaining requirements), sha256
\f{cd2e08dcb56f5716b06981e096c3c93fd417abab1989b5d22d45befeafbc383f} in \f{data/gold/GOLD_SHA256.txt}.

\textbf{Real form queries.} \nforms{} consenting responses were imported (\nformsexcluded{} excluded), \nformsvone{}
from form v1 and \nformsvtwo{} from v2. v2 added a description and three example questions after respondents were
unsure what to ask, so later replies may echo those examples; each row carries its \f{form_version}. Real questions
differ from templates: they are informal (``sem 3 of m.tech EE,SPML stream''), give course counts instead of codes,
and ask things outside the three intents (proficiency transcript, graduating by a date, other-department courses).
By kind: \nformsremaining{} remaining requirements, \nformselective{} elective advice, \nformseligibility{}
eligibility, \nformsother{} other.

original strategy and its numbers are kept unchanged and the stratified one is reported as a later addition.

\section{Part E: Versioning and splits}
DVC tracks the frozen human and raw inputs with \f{dvc add} (\f{data/raw/sources}, both course-plan folders, the raw
form CSV, the LLM cache \f{data/interim/llm_cache.jsonl}, and the round 1 and 2 tasks, manifests, exports and adjudication files) and the pipeline in \f{dvc.yaml}
(18 stages from \f{extract_sources} to \f{build_metadata}; EE fetching is frozen). Evidence files are outputs with
\f{cache: false} so they stay readable in git. \f{dvc.lock} pins the hash of every stage output. A full \f{dvc repro}
runs offline (LLM stages replay the cache) and reproduced courses, rules, students, queries, paraphrases,
augmented pairs, splits, audited labels, the gold set and base-model answers byte for byte; \f{data/metadata.csv}
differs only in its \f{collected_at_utc} run time (\f{supporting_files/F_reproducibility/repro_check.txt}). The remote is a local folder \f{../dvc_remote}. The freeze tag
\f{a1-final} named in \f{HANDOFF_A2.md} is \freezestatus.

Students are assigned to splits first, per programme, by student count (seed 207), and every pair is then rendered
with a template allowed in its split. One template per family (7 of 22) is test-only. All MTech-ICDT students form a
programme holdout: it is the smallest programme and the only one without streams, under EE rules. Augmented children
inherit their root parent's split. All overlap checks are zero (students across splits, train/val templates in test,
ICDT in train/val/test, duplicate ids), and no pair was dropped.

\section{Part F: Report and reproducibility}
\f{DATASET_CARD.md} lists sources, sizes, split balance, known biases and intended use (advisory model training and
label-noise study, not official advice). \f{ENVIRONMENT.md} records the machine (Ubuntu 22.04.5, Ryzen 5 2500U, CPU
only), Python 3.10.12, DVC 3.67.1, Label Studio 1.23.2 in its own venv, the model tag, digest and GGUF blob, all
eight seeds, and the three LLM backends. \f{LABELLING_PROTOCOL.md} records who labelled what, config and guideline
per round, item order and seed, what annotators could see, the adjudication process and a timeline matched to export
files. Exports are never edited. Rebuild:
\f{python3.10 -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt}, then \f{.venv/bin/dvc pull},
\f{.venv/bin/dvc repro} and \f{.venv/bin/pytest -q} (252 tests pass, \f{supporting_files/F_reproducibility/pytest.txt}). LLM answers are replayed from \f{data/interim/llm_cache.jsonl},
keyed by prompt id, input hash and model digest, so a rerun gives the same text.

\section{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\item Students are synthetic and valid by construction: no failed courses, substitutions or committee exceptions.
\item The sources state no enforceable PG prerequisite, so eligibility mostly tests offering and already-taken rules.
\item Kappa rests on 40 items per round; the CIs are wide and overlap.
\item LLM backends are mixed: Ollama 0.1.38 could not load the qwen2 tokenizer, so the same GGUF ran on llama.cpp, and
  part of the paraphrases ran on a Kaggle T4 with transformers fp16. Each row records its backend
  (\f{para_backend}); outputs are not bit-identical across backends.
\item The form examples were added mid-collection (v1 to v2), which may pull later wording toward them. We have
  \nforms{} real questions, below the target of 30 at the time of writing.
\item The EE site marks the ICDT discipline list ``to be updated soon'', so ICDT answers rest on a partial list.
\end{itemize}

\section{Guidelines v2 and label levels}\label{app:c}
\textbf{Label levels, in this order:} 1) decision: accept, edit, reject; 2) error category (one, by priority:
hallucinated\_course, wrong\_fact\_or\_policy, meaning\_drift, incomplete\_justification, ambiguous\_query, fluency;
or none); 3) edit text, only for edit.

\textbf{Changes from v1 to v2} (\f{labelstudio/guidelines_v2.md}, sources in round-1 \f{disagreement_analysis.md}):
\begin{itemize}
\item R1: a justification must state the gold outcome and every number, course and open item; pure encouragement is
  incomplete\_justification.
\item R2: any number or status that contradicts gold or is not in gold is wrong\_fact\_or\_policy, including vague
  text that implies a wrong status.
\item R3: edit when the fix touches at most two sentences or one query phrase, else reject; a missing fact alone is
  never a reason to reject.
\item R4: added scope, ``available'' for ``remaining'', or a question turned back on the student is meaning\_drift.
\item R5: with two problems, pick the category by priority and fix both texts.
\item R6: gold is the truth; do not correct it from memory, flag ``GOLD?'' instead.
\item R7: formatting and tone are not errors; edit or reject always needs a category other than none.
\end{itemize}
The label set and the Label Studio config (\f{labelstudio/config_v1.xml}) did not change.
